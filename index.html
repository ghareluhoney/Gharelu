Honey basics

How to actually use it

A tablespoon (about 15g) once or twice a day is the usual starting point — stirred into warm (not boiling) water, spooned over curd or oats, or taken straight off the spoon. Raw honey works well as an everyday sweetener in place of refined sugar, and many people take a spoonful before bed or first thing in the morning.

For a sore throat, a spoonful on its own or mixed with warm water and lemon is the traditional go-to. Keep it away from boiling liquids — high heat breaks down the natural enzymes that make raw honey different from the processed kind. Not recommended for children under one year of age, and if you're diabetic or on medication, it's worth checking with a doctor before making it a daily habit.

From the ridgelines

A look at where it comes from

Three ridgelines, a short walk apart from each other but each with its own flowers, light, and weather — which is exactly why the honey from one never tastes quite like the honey from another. These are the hives and the hands behind every jar.

How it's kept

Three rules we don't bend on

Cold-extracted, always

Never heated above hive temperature, so the pollen, enzymes, and aroma stay exactly as the bees left them.

One apiary per jar

Every batch is traceable to a single ridge and harvest week — no blending between sites or seasons.

Hand-labeled, numbered

Each label is written by hand and numbered against our harvest log — you can ask us exactly which week yours came from.
